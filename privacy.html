MyClock Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 4, 2026

MyClock is a clock, weather, alarm and world-clock app. It has no account and no sign-in, and its purpose is to be a display: everything you set up in it — alarms, cities, preferences — stays on your device.

Information We Collect

We do not collect, store or share your alarms, city lists, clock settings or any other personal information. All of it is kept in the app's private storage on your device. The app itself uses no analytics or crash-reporting services, and no server of ours receives anything from your device.

Location and Weather

The weather follows your current location, so with your permission the app reads your coordinates when it fetches a forecast:

  • The coordinates are sent to Open-Meteo, the weather service the forecast comes from, as part of that request — nothing else is sent, no identifiers and no account. Open-Meteo states that it collects no personal data from API use; see its privacy terms.
  • The coordinates are also handed to Apple's system location services for reverse geocoding — that is how the weather area gets the name of the place it shows.

Your location is read only while you are using the app, is never stored on a server of ours, and is never used for advertising or tracking. If you refuse location permission the app works normally — you simply won't see the weather.

Permissions Explanation

The app asks for the following permissions to provide its features:

  • Location (while using the app) — to fetch the local weather forecast and the name of your city, as described above.
  • Notifications — to deliver alarms. Alarms are scheduled as local notifications on your device; they are not push messages, and no server is involved.
  • App Tracking Transparency — used for advertising, described below.

Nothing else is requested. The microphone, camera, photo library and contacts are never used.

Advertising

MyClock displays banner ads through Google AdMob (the Google Mobile Ads SDK) on its weather, alarm and world-clock tabs — never over the clock display and never in Settings. To request, deliver and measure ads, and to detect invalid traffic, Google and its advertising partners may collect:

  • Device identifiers, including the advertising identifier (IDFA) when you allow tracking
  • IP address, and approximate location derived from it
  • Ad interaction data, such as which ads were shown, viewed or tapped
  • Diagnostic data related to ad delivery

On first launch the app asks for permission to track (Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt). If you allow it, ads may be personalised using your advertising identifier. If you decline, the app still shows ads, but they are not personalised. You can change this at any time in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, a consent form is presented before any ads are requested. You can change your tracking choice at any time in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.

For details on how Google uses data from apps that use its services, see How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services and the Google Privacy Policy.

Data Retention and Deletion

Your alarms, clock style and other preferences, and the last weather reading, are kept in the app's private storage on your device for as long as you use the app. Deleting MyClock removes all of them and cancels any scheduled alarm notifications. Because we never receive any of it, there is nothing for us to delete on a server.

Children's Privacy

MyClock is not directed to children under 13 (or the minimum age that applies in your country), and we do not knowingly collect information from children.

Changes to This Policy

We may update this policy from time to time; the date at the top will be revised when we do.
